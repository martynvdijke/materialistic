MATERIALISTIC FOR HACKER NEWS — PRIVACY NOTICE (EU/EEA/GDPR)

Effective date: 1 November 2026
Last updated: 1 November 2026

This privacy notice explains how the Android application “Materialistic for
Hacker News” (the “app”) handles personal data. It applies to the Flutter build
distributed under application ID xyz.vandijke.materialistic and to the native
build distributed under application ID io.github.hidroh.materialistic.

The app is an open-source Hacker News client. It has no developer-operated
backend, no user accounts with the publisher, no advertising, and no developer
analytics or crash-reporting SDK. The publisher does not receive app data
directly.

2. WHAT THE APP PROCESSES, WHERE, AND WHY

A. Data kept only on your device

The app stores the following locally:
- Your optional Hacker News username and password, so the app can act on your
  behalf when you log in, vote, comment, or submit. On Android the credentials
  are kept in the platform account store (native app) or in encrypted secure
  storage backed by the Android Keystore (Flutter app). They are not sent to the
  publisher.
- Favorites/saved stories, read history, search history, cached reader content,
  and app settings.

This local processing is necessary to provide the app’s core features at your
request (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)) and is based on the publisher’s legitimate
interest in operating and securing the app (Article 6(1)(f)). Local data is not
accessible to the publisher.

B. Data sent directly to Hacker News

When you use Hacker News features, the app communicates directly with Hacker
News servers:
- The official Hacker News Firebase API (https://hacker-news.firebaseio.com) is
  used to load stories, comments, and profiles. Requests include ordinary
  technical data such as your IP address and user agent, but no Hacker News
  credentials.
- The Hacker News website (https://news.ycombinator.com) is used for login,
  voting, commenting, submitting, and account-related actions. If you log in or
  post, your Hacker News username and password (or session data) are sent
  directly to Hacker News over HTTPS. Hacker News receives this data as an
  independent service and controller; its own privacy practices apply.

These transfers are necessary to perform the features you request (Article
6(1)(b)) and to operate the app securely (Article 6(1)(f)).

C. Search

Search queries are sent to the Algolia Hacker News Search API
(https://hn.algolia.com) to return results. Algolia receives the query and
ordinary technical data such as your IP address. Search history is stored only
on your device. This processing is necessary for the search feature you request
(Article 6(1)(b)) and for the publisher’s legitimate interest in providing search
(Article 6(1)(f)).

D. Reader / readability service

If the optional reader feature is configured and you choose to open an article in
the reader, the article URL may be sent to the Mercury Web Parser API
(https://mercury.postlight.com) to extract readable content. Mercury receives the
URL and ordinary technical data such as your IP address. If you do not use the
reader, no article URL is sent to Mercury. This processing occurs at your request
(Article 6(1)(b)); where consent is required, your decision to use the reader
provides that consent (Article 6(1)(a)).

E. Optional feedback to GitHub

The app has an optional feedback form. If you choose to send feedback, the app
sends your feedback title and text, together with your device manufacturer and
model, Android SDK level, and app version, to GitHub (https://github.com) and
creates a public issue in the project’s GitHub repository. Feedback is public;
do not include sensitive personal data. This transfer occurs only when you
submit the form (Article 6(1)(a), consent, or Article 6(1)(b) if you request the
support service). GitHub is an independent service and its privacy practices
apply.

F. Websites you open

If you tap a link or open an article, the app hands the URL to your browser or an
in-app browser. The destination website and your browser process that request
under their own privacy policies. The publisher does not receive that browsing
data.

3. RECIPIENTS AND INTERNATIONAL TRANSFERS

The publisher does not sell or rent personal data and does not share data with
advertisers, analytics providers, or data brokers. The recipients are the
third-party services described above:
- Hacker News / Y Combinator (United States)
- Algolia (United States and/or France)
- Mercury Web Parser / Postlight (United States), if the reader is configured
- GitHub / Microsoft (United States), only for feedback you submit
- Websites you choose to open

These recipients may process data outside the EU/EEA, mainly in the United
States. Where required, transfers rely on appropriate safeguards such as the
European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses and/or the recipient’s
certification under the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework. The publisher does not
control these third parties; their own safeguards and privacy notices apply.
You can contact the publisher using the details above for more information about
the safeguards used.

4. RETENTION

- On-device data (credentials, favorites, read history, search history, cached
  content, settings) remains on your device until you clear it in the app, clear
  the app’s storage in Android settings, or uninstall the app. Logging out
  removes the stored Hacker News credentials.
- Hacker News retains account, post, vote, and comment data according to its own
  policies. The publisher cannot access or delete Hacker News data.
- Algolia and Mercury retain request data according to their own policies.
- GitHub retains public feedback issues until they are deleted. You can edit or
  request deletion of your issue through GitHub, or contact the publisher using
  the details above.

The publisher does not maintain a copy of any of this data.

5. YOUR RIGHTS

If GDPR applies, you have the right to:
- access your personal data;
- rectify inaccurate data;
- erase your data (“right to be forgotten”);
- restrict processing;
- object to processing based on legitimate interests;
- data portability;
- withdraw consent at any time, without affecting the lawfulness of processing
  before withdrawal;
- lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority.

Because the publisher holds no server-side data about you, many requests can be
fulfilled directly on your device (for example by clearing history or logging
out) or by contacting the relevant third party. Requests concerning Hacker News
account data must be made to Hacker News. Requests concerning GitHub feedback
can be made through GitHub or to the publisher. You can contact the publisher at
the privacy contact above. You also have the right to complain to your local
data protection authority. If the publisher is established in the Netherlands,
the lead authority may be the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens.

6. CHILDREN

The app is not directed to children under 16 and the publisher does not
knowingly collect personal data from children under 16. Hacker News content and
linked websites may not be suitable for children. If you believe a child has
provided personal data to the publisher, contact the privacy contact above.

7. SECURITY

The app uses HTTPS for network requests and relies on Android’s account store or
encrypted secure storage for Hacker News credentials. The app does not log
credentials. No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, so
absolute security cannot be guaranteed.

8. NO ADS, NO SALE, NO DEVELOPER ANALYTICS

The app contains no advertising SDK, no developer-operated analytics, no
crash-reporting SDK, and no tracking. The app does not use automated
decision-making or profiling. The publisher does not sell personal data.
Google Play itself may collect install, crash, and performance data under
Google’s own terms; that data is controlled by Google, not the publisher.

9. DELETING YOUR DATA AND YOUR HACKER NEWS ACCOUNT

- Log out in the app to remove the stored Hacker News username and password from
  your device (native: account settings / log out; Flutter: Settings → Delete
  local account data or the account/logout action).
- Clear read history, search history, and favorites in the app’s Settings/Data
  section.
- Uninstall the app, or clear its storage in Android Settings → Apps →
  Materialistic → Storage, to remove all local app data.
- The app cannot delete your Hacker News account. Hacker News account deletion is
  handled by Hacker News, not by this app. See the Hacker News FAQ at
  https://news.ycombinator.com/newsfaq.html and contact hn@ycombinator.com for
  privacy or account-deletion requests. You can also review your account at
  https://news.ycombinator.com/user.

  optional, not used for tracking, and not sold.
- No advertising, no developer analytics, no crash reporting, no data sale.
